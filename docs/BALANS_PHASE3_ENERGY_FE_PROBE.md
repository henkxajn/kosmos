# BALANS 1.0 — Phase 3 · energy:Fe relation PROBE (read-only map)

> **Status: PROBE. Nothing was changed** — zero constants, zero code, zero balance. This document answers
> "what would Filip be changing, and would it do anything" *before* the magnitude question is asked.
> Evidence is file:line against the tree at the time of writing.

Prior findings this builds on: `docs/BALANS_PHASE2_PRICES.md` §2–2.1 (the two disagreeing lenses, the ×3.0
pin) and `docs/BALANS_PHASE2_ROI.md` §3.1/§9 (the 71% component share, and the warning that every power
plant's ROI hangs on `BASE_PRICE.energy`).

---

## Summary — the four answers in one paragraph each

1. **The relation is a literal 1:1 table entry** (`Fe: 1`, `energy: 1`), but the relation *as played* is not
   controllable from that entry, because the effective price is `base × scarcityMultiplier` and energy's
   multiplier is a **constant 3.0** while Fe's swings 0.2–3.0. The ratio in play therefore swings between
   ×1 and ×15 regardless of what the table says. §1.
2. **A static change would not be masked — it would be inert.** The pin is a *factor*, not a clamp: setting
   `energy: 3` yields 9.0 Kr, linearly. But the code paths that would spend those Kr never execute for
   energy, because energy is a flow with a permanent stock of 0. B1 and B2 are **mechanically separable,
   practically coupled**. §2.
3. **Blast radius is almost nil, and points the wrong way.** Energy appears in **no recipe and no build
   cost** — raising its price cannot make a single building or component more expensive. Its one live
   credit-moving consumer is the emergency-buy button, which is itself a no-op purchase. The one place a
   price change *will* visibly move numbers is **the harness's own ROI ruler**. §3.
4. **Five candidate control points**, of which only one (flow-aware scarcity) makes the number mean
   anything, and only one (energy priced into production) makes energy *felt* in build cost. §4.

## 1. Where the energy:Fe relation actually lives

### 1.1 Static layer — a literal entry, and a leaf with no downstream

`src/data/TradeValuesData.js`:

| line | entry |
|---|---|
| `:17` | `Fe:   1,` |
| `:30` | `energy: 1,` |

Both are **hand-written literals**, not derived. The table's stated derivation rule (`:13` — *"koszt
surowców w recepturze × 1.3"*) applies only to commodities; raw ores and the three base resources
(`food`, `water`, `energy`) are leaves that the rule consumes but never produces.

**Critical structural fact: energy is in no recipe.** `grep energy src/data/CommoditiesData.js` → **0 hits**.
Recipes are pure material (`structural_alloys` = `{ Fe: 8, C: 4 }`, `CommoditiesData.js:25`). So energy sits
*outside* the derivation graph entirely: changing `BASE_PRICE.energy` propagates to **no other price in the
table**. Changing `BASE_PRICE.Fe`, by contrast, would invalidate the documented arithmetic of ~20 commodity
prices. The two ends of this "relation" are not symmetric objects.

### 1.2 Dynamic layer — how a price is actually computed

`TradeValuesData.js:83-108` — `scarcityMultiplier(stock, annualConsumption)`, two branches:

- **flow branch** (`:89-98`), taken when `annualConsumption > 0.01`: `y = stock / annualConsumption` (years of
  supply), piecewise-linear `y>10 → 0.2` … `y→0 → 3.0`, terminal `return 3.0` at `:97`.
- **stock-threshold branch** (`:100-107`), for goods with no running consumption: `stock<=0 → 3.0` … `>=20 → 0.3`.

The two layers combine multiplicatively at exactly one production entry point:

```js
// CivilianTradeSystem.js:957-960
getLocalPrice(goodId, colony) {
  const stock = colony?.resourceSystem?.inventory?.get(goodId) ?? 0;
  return (BASE_PRICE[goodId] ?? 1) * scarcityMultiplier(stock, this._getConsumption(goodId, colony));
}
```

`_getConsumption` (`:606-617`) sums negative producer rates — for energy this is non-zero in any colony with
buildings, so energy always takes the **flow** branch.

### 1.3 The complete consumer set of `BASE_PRICE` in production code

Four files, seven call sites (`grep -rn BASE_PRICE src --include=*.js`, excluding `src/testing`):

| site | what it does | uses scarcity? |
|---|---|---|
| `CivilianTradeSystem.js:294` | trade-capacity budgeting of a candidate transfer | no — static |
| `CivilianTradeSystem.js:342-345` | `tcCost = qty × price` charged to both colonies | no — static |
| `CivilianTradeSystem.js:348-355` | Kr generated by a transfer (exporter 6%, importer 3%) | yes |
| `CivilianTradeSystem.js:959` | `getLocalPrice` — the nominal single price source | yes |
| `TradeOrderBoard.js:72-75` | price lock when a cross-empire order is placed | yes (via `getLocalPrice`) |
| `TradeOverlay.js:393, 462-463` | market rows / buy-sell panel display | yes (via `getLocalPrice`) |
| `TradeOverlay.js:747` | **emergency-buy cost** — the only button where a player pays Kr derived from this table | yes |

⚠ **The "one source of truth" comment at `CivilianTradeSystem.js:955-956` is not accurate.**
`TradeOverlay._calcLocalPrices` (`:1002-1024`) re-implements the formula locally — it reads `inventory`,
sums producer rates and calls `scarcityMultiplier` itself, bypassing `getLocalPrice`. Any change to the
pricing formula must be made in **both** places or the UI and the economy will disagree. This matters
directly for option B in §4.

### 1.4 Verdict on question 1

There **is** a single number (`TradeValuesData.js:30`), but it does not control the relation. The relation as
experienced is:

```
price(energy) = BASE_PRICE.energy × 3.0          ← multiplier constant, always
price(Fe)     = BASE_PRICE.Fe     × [0.2 … 3.0]  ← multiplier responds to stock
```

so the *ratio* is set by three interacting things: the two table entries, the pin on energy (§2), and Fe's
in-play stock level. It is **partly a constant, partly emergent** — which is exactly why the panel measured
×15 in play against a table that says ×1.

## 2. The flow-vs-inventory problem — and whether it masks a static change

### 2.1 Why the stock is structurally 0, not incidentally 0

`src/systems/ResourceSystem.js`:

- `:52-65` — `inventory` is populated from `MINED_RESOURCES` + `HARVESTED_RESOURCES` + `COMMODITIES`. Energy is
  in none of those sets; **no `inventory` key for energy is ever created**.
- `:67-73` — energy lives in a separate object: `this.energy = { production, consumption, balance, brownout }`.
- `:156`, `:163` (`spend`), `:180` (`receive`), `:196` (`canAfford`) — every inventory mutation path explicitly
  `continue`s on energy (*"energia to flow, nie pobieramy jednorazowo"*).
- `:222-227` (`serialize`) writes energy outside `inventory`; `:392-402` (`restore`) only ever writes keys that
  came from `inventory`. So a save cannot reintroduce one either.

Therefore `getLocalPrice('energy')` reads `inventory.get('energy') ?? 0` → **0, under every reachable state**.

### 2.2 Both branches pin to the maximum

- consumption > 0 (any colony with buildings) → flow branch, `y = 0 / c = 0` → falls past every threshold →
  `return 3.0` (`TradeValuesData.js:97`).
- consumption = 0 (hypothetical colony with no energy draw) → stock branch, `stock <= 0 → return 3.0` (`:102`).

**There is no state in which energy is not 3.0.** The PRICES panel confirmed this empirically: the set of
distinct energy prices across 368 samples is `{3}`.

### 2.3 Answer to question 2 — separable, but not independently useful

**Would a static change take effect?** Yes, arithmetically. The pin is a multiplicative factor, not a ceiling:

| `BASE_PRICE.energy` | effective price |
|---|---|
| 1 (today) | 3.0 Kr |
| 3 | 9.0 Kr |
| 8 | 24.0 Kr |

Nothing clamps it. So **B1 does not require B2 to be fixed first in order to move the number.**

**Would it do anything?** Almost nothing, because the paths that consume the price never fire for energy:

- Energy *is* in `TRADEABLE_GOODS` (`TradeValuesData.js:136-139` filters only `research`) and *is* scanned every
  routing tick (`CivilianTradeSystem.js:254`).
- `_deficitScore` (`:569-587`) returns the **maximum 10** for energy always (`ratio = 0` → `1/0.1`, `:585-586`) —
  energy looks like the most desperate shortage in the game, permanently.
- `_surplusScore` (`:553-567`) returns **0** for energy always (`0 / (c×10)`, `:566`) — nobody can ever supply it.
- Consequently `_pickTransfers` computes `surplus = 0` (`:305-321`) and `_executeTransfer` early-returns on
  `available = 0` (`:334-336`). **No energy transfer ever executes**, so neither the TC charge nor the Kr
  generation ever runs at energy's price.

**So: mechanically separable, practically coupled.** You can raise the number today and it will surface in
exactly two places (a market row nobody can trade, and one broken button — §3.2). If the intent is for
energy's price to *mean* something — respond to grid headroom, drive routing, be worth defending — then
B2 is the prerequisite, not the follow-up.

## 3. Blast radius — what would actually move

### 3.1 Energy is not a priced input anywhere in production

- **Recipes:** zero energy (§1.1). Components are ore-only by construction.
- **Build costs:** no building has `cost: { energy }`. Buildings carry `energyCost` — a *per-year operating
  drain*, documented at `BuildingsData.js:8` and present on ~40 entries — which `BuildingSystem.js:1972-1974`
  converts into a **negative energy rate**, i.e. into the flow balance, never into a payable cost.
- **Factories:** energy enters production as a *throughput gate*, not a price. `ResourceSystem.getEnergyAvailability()`
  (`:409`) scales factory output; `FactorySystem.js:862-868` notes deliberately that a throttled factory still
  pays full energy — the MDA loop "shortage → penalty → build a power plant".

**Consequence:** raising energy's price **cannot** make any building or component more expensive. The ROI
slice's 71%-in-components wall is ore-denominated by construction; there is no energy term in it to inflate.
The answer to "is energy priced into component recipes at all, or only into operating balance" is: **only
operating balance, and not even as a price — as a flow constraint.**

### 3.2 The one live credit-moving consumer is broken

`TradeOverlay.js:742-757` — "Awaryjny zakup": picks the good with the **highest** scarcity multiplier
(`scarce.sort((a,b) => b.mult - a.mult)`, `:744-745`) and charges
`BASE_PRICE[id] × mult × 50 × 0.15`.

Because energy is pinned at the maximum possible multiplier (3.0) and passes both filters in
`_calcLocalPrices` (`:1016` consumption > 0, `:1019` mult > 1.1), **energy is the default target of that
button** in a typical colony. Current cost: `1 × 3.0 × 50 × 0.15 = 23 Kr`.

The handler (`:1088-1097`) then does `resSys.receive({ energy: 50 })` — and `receive` **skips energy**
(`ResourceSystem.js:180`). **The player pays credits and receives nothing.**

This is a defect independent of the price question, but it is the single place where `BASE_PRICE.energy`
moves real credits — so it is worth stating plainly: *raising energy's base price would make a no-op button
proportionally more expensive* (at `energy: 3` it becomes 68 Kr).

### 3.3 The market row is dead, not exploitable

`TradeOverlay.js:388-404` iterates `TRADEABLE_GOODS`, so energy is listed (stock 0, ~3 Kr) and clickable. An
order placed on it always fails at settlement: both sides check `inventory.get(g) < qty`
(`TradeOrderBoard.js:143` buy, `:150` sell) → `insufficient_goods` → cancelled. No credits move. Cosmetic
clutter, not an exploit.

### 3.4 ⚠ The instrument moves with the constant — read this before designing the experiment

The ROI harness converts building output to Kr using **this same table** (`RoiTelemetry.js:33-40` header,
`:88-91` `priceOf`). Every power plant's payback is denominated in `BASE_PRICE.energy`.

**Raise energy to N and every power plant's measured ROI improves ~N×, with zero change in the game.**
`BALANS_PHASE2_ROI.md` §9 already flagged this: *"the entire value of every power plant hangs on that single
number"*. A naive harness diff across a price change is therefore **not** a clean before/after — part of the
movement is the ruler, not the thing measured. A follow-up experiment has to either hold the ROI valuation
fixed at the old table or report both rulers side by side.

### 3.5 Net answer to question 3

The economy does **not** tilt toward "energy matters" when energy's price rises. Price and physical scarcity
are wired to different subsystems: energy already matters *physically* (brownout gate, staffing-scaled draw,
plant siting) and matters *economically* essentially nowhere. Raising the price without §4-B or §4-C changes
display, one broken button, and the measuring instrument.

## 4. Control points — the options, laid out, none applied

First, separate three targets that "make energy expensive relative to Fe" could mean:

- **T1 — the number is honest.** The table stops claiming 1:1 for a pair the game treats as 15:1.
- **T2 — the price is a signal.** Energy's price responds to grid headroom instead of being a constant.
- **T3 — energy is felt in cost.** Building and manufacturing get more expensive when energy is scarce.

| option | change | serves | cost / risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Bump `BASE_PRICE.energy`** | 1 line, `TradeValuesData.js:30` | T1 | trivial edit; **behaviourally inert** (§2.3); inflates the broken button (§3.2); shifts the ROI ruler (§3.4) |
| **B. Flow-aware scarcity** | `scarcityMultiplier` + `getLocalPrice:957` + the UI duplicate `TradeOverlay:1002-1024` | T2 (and makes A meaningful) | medium; must fix both formula copies or they diverge; changes routing scores (energy stops being a permanent max-deficit); decide whether energy stays in `TRADEABLE_GOODS` since it still cannot physically move |
| **C. Price energy into production** | energy term in recipes or a per-craft energy charge in `FactorySystem` | T3 — the only path that touches the 71% wall | **structural**: `ResourceSystem.spend/canAfford` refuse energy by design (`:156/:163/:196`); needs a flow-charge model, not a constant. Highest payoff, highest blast radius |
| **D. Move physical scarcity instead** | plant `rates.energy` (8/18/25) vs ~40 `energyCost` entries, tech multipliers | tightness, not the price relation | pure constants, harness-measurable, no structural risk; **sidesteps the audit finding rather than answering it** |
| **E. Re-base the table** | declare the unit and re-derive | T1 at the root | largest; see below |

**On E — the deeper reading of the finding.** The table mixes two incompatible units: utilities are priced
per *flow-year* (energy has no stock to price), ore is priced per *ton in store*. `energy: 1` and `Fe: 1` are
not "the same value", they are **not the same kind of quantity**. That, rather than the choice of number, is
why the two lenses can point in opposite directions without either being wrong. Any magnitude picked for A
inherits this ambiguity.

**Framing for the decision (not a decision):**

- **A alone answers nothing measurable** — it changes a display and a broken button, and it moves the ruler
  the experiment would be read with.
- **B is the smallest change that makes the number mean something**, and it is what makes a later A worth
  doing at all. It is also self-contained: one function, two call sites.
- **C is the only route to "energy matters in build cost"** — and it is a design change, not a tuning pass.
- **D is the cheapest way to make the player *feel* energy scarcity**, and is orthogonal to the price
  question; it can be done independently, at any time.

**Pre-flight checks for whichever is chosen:** (i) the harness ROI ruler (§3.4) — decide how it is held;
(ii) the duplicated pricing formula (§1.3) — fix both or neither; (iii) the emergency-buy no-op (§3.2) —
worth fixing regardless, and best fixed *before* a price change so it does not silently get worse.
